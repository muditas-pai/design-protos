# Voice and Tone Guide

The master reference for all product copy in these protos. Every string we
ship (tooltips, modals, buttons, empty states, checkout) follows this guide.

## Our Voice

We're the brilliant but humble expert who makes complex things feel simple.
We work alongside our users, not above them.

## Core Personality

- Humble and straightforward
- Mature expert
- Mildly encouraging without sycophancy
- Collaborative ("we" language)
- Can be playful at the right moments

## Writing Principles

### 1. Always Be Specific, Never Vague

Tell users exactly what will happen next.

- No: "Let's get started"
- Yes: "Let's start building your presentation"
- No: "Tell us about yourself"
- Yes: "What's your role? This helps us suggest the right templates"

### 2. Use Short, Direct Sentences

Get to the point quickly with one idea per sentence.

- No: "Our Pro plan includes unlimited AI-powered presentation generation, the ability to export to PowerPoint format, access to premium templates, and priority processing so your presentations are created faster."
- Yes: "Pro includes unlimited presentations. Export to PowerPoint. Get priority AI. Most professionals choose this."

### 3. Add Brief Context When Helpful

Tell users why you're asking or saying something, but keep it short.

- No: "Enter your company website" (too minimal)
- No: "Enter your company website so we can analyze your brand identity, extract color palettes, and ensure visual consistency across all your presentations" (too much)
- Yes: "Enter your company website so we can match your brand"

### 4. Use "We" Language

We're in this together. We build presentations with our users.

- No: "You can create a presentation" / Yes: "We'll create your presentation"
- No: "The AI will generate slides" / Yes: "We'll generate your slides"

### 5. Handle Failures Gracefully

Stay calm and solution-focused.

- No: "AI failed to generate your presentation"
- Yes: "We're having trouble with this request. Let's try a different approach"
- No: "Error: Processing failed"
- Yes: "Something went wrong on our end. We're fixing it now"

### 6. Use Active Voice, Not Passive

- No: "Your slides will be created shortly" / Yes: "We'll create your slides in 30 seconds"
- No: "An email will be sent to you" / Yes: "We'll email you when it's ready"

### 7. Talk Benefits, Not Just Features

Focus on what users accomplish, not what our technology does.

- No: "Our AI analyzes your content and applies design algorithms"
- Yes: "We'll organize your ideas and make them look professional"
- No: "Advanced template generation with brand matching"
- Yes: "We'll match your company colors and fonts automatically"

### 8. Stay Consistent with Terminology

- No: using "presentation," "deck," "slides," and "PPT" interchangeably
- Yes: one primary term ("presentation"), used everywhere
- No: "AI assistant," "our system," "the algorithm"
- Yes: consistent "we" language throughout

### 9. Use Positive Framing

Guide users forward instead of pointing out what's missing.

- No: "You haven't selected a template yet"
- Yes: "Choose your template to get started"

### 10. Be Playful When Appropriate

Playful during: onboarding, step-by-step creation, pricing conversations.
Professional during: errors, technical explanations, payment, final exports.

- No: "Oops! Something totally bonked out on us!"
- Yes: "Something went wrong on our end. We're fixing it now"
- No: "Let's make some magic happen!" (during creation)
- Yes: "This is looking good already" (during creation)

### 11. Adjust Tone by User Persona Wherever Possible

Business professionals: slightly more formal, focus on efficiency.
Students and educators: slightly more encouraging, focus on learning.

## Anti-principles

1. **No sycophancy or vague enthusiasm.** Not "Your idea is brilliant!" but "Let's turn this into a compelling presentation."
2. **No jargon, buzzwords, or corporate speak.** Not "Leverage our cutting-edge AI" but "We'll organize your ideas and make them visual."
3. **No hedging language.** Not "We might be able to help" but "We'll create a presentation that works for your audience."
4. **No unnecessary apologies.** Not "Sorry, but you'll need to upgrade to export" but "Ready to export? Here are your options."
5. **No false urgency.** Not "Act now! Limited time offer!" but "Most professionals choose the annual plan."
