Naming

Four kinds of class, and every component is built from them in the same order: the component, then what it is, then how big, then what state it is in.

The four

KindFormExampleRule
Component.pai-<component>.pai-avatarOne noun. Always required.
Variant.pai-<component>-<variant>.pai-badge-successWhat it is. Tone, shape, emphasis.
Size.pai-<component>-<size>.pai-switcher-smallsmall · medium · large. Omitted means medium.
State.is-<state>.is-errorWhat is true right now. Set and removed at runtime.

Variant against state

The distinction decides which form a class takes, and it is the one most often got wrong. A variant is chosen when the screen is built and does not change. A badge is a success badge; it will not become a danger badge. A state is true now and false later — a field is in error until it is corrected, a menu is open until it closes. Variants read as part of the component's name; states read as something happening to it.

Two rules that still hold

  • Put .is-interactive on a real button, link or label. It supplies cursor, hover and a focus ring — it cannot make a div focusable, and a div you can click is unreachable by keyboard.
  • Do not nest a card in a card. The inner one claims to be a separate thing you can act on, and it is not. Nesting a surface in a surface is a different question — white on white needs the raised step.

Surface

Every plane a screen can put something on. A surface is not a colour — it is a background, an elevation and a stacking layer travelling together, and they have to travel together or the illusion breaks: a white box with no shadow at modal height reads as a hole in the page, and a shadow on the page background reads as a smudge.

Card and raised are both white with a shadow, and the difference is what they sit on. A card sits on the page and needs only enough lift to separate from grey. Raised sits on another white surface — a panel inside a modal — and needs more, because white on white has nothing but the shadow to say there are two planes.

Why eight, not the obvious seven

A side panel is not its own surface. It is the modal surface anchored to an edge — same background, same elevation, same layer, different geometry. Naming it would mean two things to keep in step for no difference a reader can see.

Two are easy to leave out and both earn a place. Sunken is the only surface that goes the other way; it is not a darker card, because nothing is meant to look like it is sitting on it — it is meant to look cut in. And scrim is a surface even though nothing rests on it: it is what makes the thing above it legible as above.

The layer scale

--z-base 0  ·  --z-raised 10  ·  --z-overlay 100  ·  --z-scrim 1000  ·  --z-modal 1010  ·  --z-toast 2000  ·  --z-tooltip 3000

The layer is the part this system was missing, and the corpus shows why: it carries z-index values of 0, 1, 2, 20, 30, 60, 99, 500, 999, 9999 and 2147483000 — which is what happens when every screen picks its own.

The gaps are deliberate, so something new can slot between two layers without renumbering everything above it. Modal sits above scrim by number rather than by DOM order, so a modal rendered before its own scrim still lands on top. Tooltip is highest because a tooltip can be triggered from inside a toast, and a toast can appear over a modal.

What it collapses at, and why those numbers

900px and 600px are the only two widths the corpus has ever branched on. They are not invented device sizes: 900 is where the analytics modal drops its two columns to one, and 600 is where the create flow does. A grid that collapses where the screens around it already do disappears; one with breakpoints of its own fights them.

Three and four halve to two at 900 before going to one at 600, so a four-up does not jump straight to a single column and double the page height. A grid that must not collapse sets --cols inline, which beats every rule above it — no opt-out class is published because nothing needs one yet.

Every column is minmax(0, 1fr) rather than 1fr. That is a fix for a bug the corpus has: a plain 1fr will not shrink below its content, so one long unbroken string — a file name, a URL — pushes the whole grid wider than its container instead of ellipsing.

How the loop is seamless

The track holds its contents twice and travels exactly −50%. At the end of the run the second copy sits precisely where the first began, so the jump back to zero lands on an identical frame and cannot be seen. Duplicate the items or the loop visibly snaps — it is the one part of this that is not optional. Mark the copies data-ticker-clone aria-hidden="true": they are scaffolding, and a screen reader should hear the list once.

The edges are a mask-image rather than a gradient laid over the top, so the strip works on whatever surface it is dropped onto — a white overlay would only disappear against white. The logos sit grey and half-lit because they are somebody else's, used as evidence, and nine at full strength out-shout the screen they are evidence for.

Under prefers-reduced-motion it stops and becomes a scrollable row, and the duplicate copies are hidden — with no loop they are just a list printed twice. An endless sideways crawl is the exact thing that setting exists for.

Chart

A column chart, a donut, and the key that reads either. Every part is placed from --at, a fraction of the axis — 0 at the baseline, 1 at the top — so a tick, its gridline and the bar it measures stay in register at any height. A bar takes --value, the same 0–1 API as .pai-progress.

pathLength="1" on each arc is what lets --value be a fraction: it tells the browser to treat the circumference as one unit, so the same markup works at any radius and nothing computes 2πr. --at is where an arc starts — the sum of the arcs before it.

Distinct colours, never shades of one. A ramp reads as an order — low to high, worst to best — and Direct link, Email and LinkedIn are not steps of anything. Two tints of one hue meeting on a curve also read as one arc with a shadow on it: hue survives a 14px stroke, lightness does not. The same goes for the key, whose swatch is 10px — a reader can name a hue at that size but cannot place a tint on a scale.

What it does not do

The chart draws what it is given and computes nothing. Scale, ticks and rounding are the caller's — whether zero belongs on the axis and whether to round to a nice number are judgements about data the component cannot see, so a component that guessed would be guessing.

Why the ratio is not negotiable

A presentation slide is 16:9 and every deck in assets/decks/ renders at 16:9, so a thumbnail at any other ratio is either letterboxing a slide or cropping one. The dashboard’s handoff drew 366.5 × 202 — 1.81:1, four pixels short, enough to letterbox every cover on the shelf.

display:block is load-bearing. aspect-ratio does nothing to an inline box, so as a bare <span> a thumbnail is sized by its content and the ratio is silently ignored. That is how the dashboard’s first covers came out at 1.32:1.

The picture is object-fit: cover, so a source that is not quite 16:9 is cropped rather than squashed. A crop loses an edge; a squash makes everything on the slide wrong.

Shimmer

Three ways content can be busy, and picking the wrong one makes the screen lie about whether there is anything to read. .pai-skeleton is a placeholder — the content is not here, and this is the shape it will take. .pai-shimmer is a state — the content is here and is being worked on, and stays readable. .pai-rewriting is the content being replaced, and deliberately does not. The first two draw the same sweep, from one definition, so they can never drift apart.

Elevation and focus

Two families. Elevation is how far off the page a surface sits; focus is that a thing is selected, which is not a depth at all — and it has to survive whatever is underneath it, which is why one ring cannot serve a white card and a photograph.

One shadow colour at three strengths — drop-1, drop-2, drop-3 — and each step adds a layer to the step below it. Every step carries the same 1px inner white highlight, so a surface catches light at its top edge whatever depth it sits at.

Near-black, never brand — blue reads as a state the component owns, black reads as the thing you have got hold of. surface and image lay a gap of the page colour down first, so the ring never touches the thing’s own edge. A dark region re-declares --bg-elevated and --border-primary-inverted, and the whole family follows it.

Colour

Two layers. A ramp step holds a value; a semantic token holds a reference to one. Components use the semantic and never the ramp. Every swatch below is read from pai.css rather than copied.

Modals

Three axes, not one list. Size sets how much of the screen it takes, orientation the aspect its content wants, and type decides whether the body sits on its own card. Every combination that means something is below — each tile drawing its footprint against the viewport at true proportion. Click one to open it.

Scrim

The layer between a modal and the page under it. .pai-scrim covers the viewport; --bg-scrim is the colour and --bg-scrim-blur the blur it puts on what is behind it. Plain black, because a tinted scrim pushes its colour through blurred content.
