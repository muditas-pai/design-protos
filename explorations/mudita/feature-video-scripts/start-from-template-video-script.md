# Start from a Template: first-fold video script

Hero video for the Start from a Template feature page. Screen recording, no face, no hands.
Written for muted autoplay and loop, so the on-screen text carries it. VO is for the long cut only.

Grounded in the JAS '26 handoff flow:
`figma.com/design/OywpgPNGvoFAIS4haGGehy/JAS--26---Handoff?node-id=1-2798`

**Claim the video lands:** this deck already answers everything a good one has to answer, and you
build on top of that.

## What the feature is actually claiming

"The structure is already decided" is the tactical version, and it undersells the thing. Twelve
slides in a fixed order is a mechanism, not a benefit. The benefit is **authority about what good
looks like**, handed over before the user starts.

The job to be done is not "give me a deck outline". It is *I need to be sure this presentation is
good, and that I have covered every point it needs to cover.* The anxiety is not a blank page. It
is walking into the room having missed something, and finding out when someone asks.

So a template is a position on what an excellent version of this deck answers. The user's own
context is what gets built on top of it. Two things follow, and both matter for how the copy is
written:

- **The authority can come from us or from the team.** Our gallery carries a view on what a good
  partnership proposal answers. A team can also save their own, so that every sales proposal or
  partnership deck the team ships covers the points they have decided matter. Same mechanism, and
  in the team case it is quiet governance rather than a starting point.
- **So the copy cannot say "we".** Lines like "we know what a great deck answers" break the moment
  the template is one the user's own team authored. Write the authority as a property of the
  template, not of Presentations.ai.

Not in this video: the team-authored / save-your-own-template case. It is the stronger enterprise
story and probably its own video, but it needs a second persona and a governance beat that a
40 second hero cut cannot hold.

## The real flow, as designed

```
1  Dashboard           prompt box + 4 quick actions, one is "Use a Template"
2  Select a template   full screen, search + 11 category tabs (Sales, Marketing,
                       Strategy, Education, Branding, Development, Customer Service,
                       Research, HR, Operations), grid of authored template cards
3  Template detail     lightbox: large slide preview + filmstrip of the deck's slides,
                       right panel with title, author, "Use this Template",
                       description, Preview / Export as PowerPoint, share row
4  Share your context  split screen. Left: "Share your own context, and we'll update
                       the template" + prompt box + Upload files or links + model
                       picker + Create Presentation. Right: the template's slides,
                       fully designed and fully populated with their own demo content
5  Context filled      same screen, the box holding an unstructured braindump.
                       Create Presentation goes live
6  Generating          "Letting it simmer… 33s" with a visible reasoning trace.
                       Right panel: slides under a loading shimmer
7  Editor              the finished deck
```

## The problem this script has to solve

Templates are never empty. They arrive fully designed and fully written with their own demo
content, so there is no "blank structure" moment anywhere in the product. The claim still has to
be made visually, which means finding the two places completeness is legible:

1. **The filmstrip in the detail lightbox (step 3).** The only place you can see what the deck
   covers before committing to it.
2. **The editor's slide rail (step 7).** The same coverage, now carrying your content. This is the
   proof, and it only works if the video showed the filmstrip earlier so the viewer can recognise
   that nothing was dropped.

Everything else in the flow is content. Those two shots carry the argument.

> **Design note worth raising separately.** In the current detail lightbox the filmstrip is small
> and cropped, so the slide titles are not readable. If the claim is that a template is a position
> on what a good deck answers, that panel probably needs a slide-outline list (titles, in order,
> scrollable) beside the thumbnails. Good for the video, and better for the feature: the outline is
> the reason to pick one template over another, and right now you cannot see it before committing.

## What she actually types

The braindump is the most important piece of writing in this video. It has to be visibly
unstructured, lowercase, with open questions still in it. The handoff mock does this well with its
travel example, so match that register:

```
ok trailhead partnership thoughts before i forget

their spring gear drop is apr 14, our cold brew season basically starts the
same week, feels too obvious not to do

audience, we're 1.4M monthly, they're 2.1M, overlap is maybe 380k? ask growth
for the real number

they have 40 stores and a 900k list which is the thing we actually want. we
have capacity (12k units) and the studio

idea: "the early start". 5am, first light, first mile, first cup. bundle is
their flask plus our beans

no cash either way, in kind, media value probably evens out around 180k each
side but finance needs to confirm

ridge cycles worked, 8.4k bundles in six weeks, 22% new to us, use that

legal will want 3 weeks so kickoff has to be feb

who owns this on their side? need a name before the meeting
```

Note which questions she never answered: no timeline, no success metrics, no next steps, and only
half of the value exchange. She did not forget them. She never got to them. The template had
already decided they were not optional. That gap is the whole video.

### Why these lines and not the earlier ones

The first draft counted slides (`Same twelve slides.`). That is the mechanism, and it makes the
feature sound like an outline generator. Every line above is written about **coverage and
completeness** instead, and none of them says "we", so the same copy holds when the template is one
the user's own team saved.

## The shot that can't be cut

**0:07 to 0:14, the filmstrip scrub.** It is the only moment in the product where you can see what
the deck sets out to answer before any of the user's content exists, so it is the only place the
claim can be made at all. If the titles are not readable at hero scale, the video is just another
generation demo and the feature reads as "AI writes a deck" rather than "this deck already knows
what good looks like".

Its payoff at **0:26**, the editor rail, only works because of it. Show the rail for a full beat
before moving to the canvas. The viewer needs a moment to register that nothing was dropped on the
way through.

## Production notes

- **Shimmer, don't freeze.** The loading state should read as work happening on the slides: a soft
  highlight sweeping across the blurred thumbnails, roughly one pass every 1.2s. A static blur
  reads as a broken image.
- **Do not cut to the editor early.** The shimmer beat is short (4s) but it has to exist, otherwise
  the transition reads as a scene change rather than the same deck arriving.
- **Match the filmstrip order to the editor rail exactly.** If a single slide moves between the two
  shots, the whole argument collapses. This is the one continuity error that matters.
- **The braindump must look typed by a person.** Lowercase, inconsistent punctuation, at least one
  unanswered question left in it. If it reads as a well-formed prompt, the contrast dies.
- **Show the category tabs long enough to register** (about a second on Marketing). They are what
  tells the viewer this is a library of deck types, not a library of colour schemes.
- **Don't put a slide count on screen.** It pulls the claim back down to mechanism. Coverage is the
  point, not quantity.
- **One CTA at the end**, navy filled. No exclamation marks in any on-screen text.
- Numbers on screen should be specific and odd (`8.4K bundles`, `roughly 380K`). Round numbers read
  as placeholder.
- Swap **Marlow** and **Trailhead** for whatever clears legal.

## Open dependencies

- The `Co-Marketing Partnership Proposal` template needs to exist in the Marketing tab, covering
  those questions. If the real gallery has no partnership template, either it gets built or the
  video moves to one that does exist.
- The filmstrip needs readable slide titles at hero scale. See the design note above.

## How it pairs with Project Knowledge

```
Start from a Template   →   what a good deck answers, decided before you type
Project Knowledge       →   the answers themselves, drawn from your own files
```

The template guarantees nothing important goes unanswered. Project Knowledge guarantees the answers
are true. Together they cover the two ways a generated deck normally fails.
