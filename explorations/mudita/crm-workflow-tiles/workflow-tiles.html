Linework between the logo and the deck

One card, eight drawings. Round 9 drew eight single connectors on eight compositions and the answer was that the lines wanted to be more interesting, not the arrangements: multiple lines, some decorative items, light grey. So this round holds the composition completely still — round 9's 01, the stepped pile at the right of a grey head with the connector logo in its slot at the left, which is the shape that has survived every round since 6 — and varies nothing but the linework. Rounds 1–9 sit beside this file as crazy8s-round1.html … -round9.html.

Eight references came with the ask and they are three families, not one. Routed (01–03): orthogonal runs with radiused corners, branching, and a node vocabulary where the shape means something — a filled circle where a line meets another, a hollow diamond where it passes a waypoint, a filled square where a run ends, and a rounded-square port for an origin. Orbital (04–05): overlapping rotated ellipses and chains of loops, with dots sitting on the curves and a lozenge as a terminus. Wave (06–08): an axis that everything is measured against, several curves of differing wavelength and phase sharing it, ellipses sitting on the axis at the crossings, registration ticks, and a bundle of strands that pinches to a point and blooms apart.

The head leaves you three narrow lanes and three narrow bands, and that is the round's real problem. Every reference draws its linework on an empty ground; ours is mostly deck. Measured off the page at three-up, where the head is 368 by 188: a 96-wide left column, a 16-wide gap column between the logo and the pile that runs clear from the card's top edge to its bottom, and three 16px bands along the top, the foot and the right edge. Everything else is behind an opaque picture. So the answer is occlusion, which is already proven here — round 7's laced variant and round 9's ribbon both found that a line going behind an object and re-emerging reads as continuous. Every variant dives under the pile and comes back out in the 16px band at the card's right edge, and the eye joins the two ends. It is what a transit map does when two lines cross.

One pixel, three greys, and no brand ink anywhere. Light grey was asked for, so it is the constraint rather than a starting point. --border-primary is a 20% near-black and computes to about #C6C8CA over the head's --bg-tertiary; --border-secondary at 9% computes to about #E1E2E3, which round 6 found is not there over a short run and which this round found is exactly right under a second line; --border-quaternary is an opaque #D4D4D4 and the only one of the three that does not change with what is behind it. Every line and every ornament in every variant says which one it takes and why.

All of it is SVG, and most of what it needs the design system does not publish. Round 9 found that construction decides what is possible — a CSS border cannot carry an arbitrary path — so each card carries one <svg> as the head's first child, which is what puts every line behind the logo and behind the pile without a z-index. Named rather than smuggled in: a path-turn radius (--rounded-* are container corners; 8 and 12 are borrowed from --rounded-lg and --rounded-xl and that is a proposal), a dash (nothing publishes one; 1 on 4, round-capped), an amplitude, a wavelength, an ellipse ratio and an angle (none of it exists), and a stroke width (the system publishes borders, not strokes).

Two hard constraints, and both are checked on the page rather than claimed here. Every variant draws at least two separate runs and at least two kinds of ornament — one line is a round 9 variant — and no two variants share the same run count and the same ornament set. Seven things the page said that the reasoning had not. Every variant was drawn, screenshotted at four times size and read back before a word of this was written, and six of the eight changed. A hollow ring and a filled dot at one token are not on the same footing — 02's ring is twice the diameter of its dots and still the weaker mark, because the eye counts ink and a ring is an outline round a hole. There is no fix inside the border ramp: --border-primary is the darkest hairline the system publishes. A drawing can connect nothing. 05's ellipse passed cleanly above the logo and cleanly below it and touched neither, which for a drawing about a connection is the one thing it may not do; it was flattened until its lower arc runs through the logo. An ornament placed on the longest visible stretch lands where the stretch is, not where the eye is — 05's five ticks all went into the eight pixels between the deck's foot and the head's edge. Five dots inside seven pixels are one smudge: 08's dots were put where each strand crosses into the deck, and measured off the page they were all at one x within seven pixels, because the bloom has forty-eight pixels to happen in. A tick on top of a dot is neither mark — two of 07's six landed on a crossing. A wave band drawn shallow runs above the logo and never touches it, so 07's is pinned to the logo now rather than typed in: crest four pixels clear of the deck, trough six pixels inside the tile. And a rule with nothing anchored on it is a divider, not an axis — 06's vertical would read as separating the logo from the deck if the ellipse were not sitting on the crossing.

All eight hold to exactly the same width, and that is the control working. Squeezed in twenty-pixel steps, every variant first breaks at a 980px window — the card is then 228 wide, and what gives is Next run: On a new account and 22 Aug, 11:40am stop fitting beside their labels on one baseline and spill past the card's edge. That is round 9's 01 breaking, not the linework. The <svg> is sized to the head and clipped by it, so no drawing can push a single pixel of layout, and eight identical numbers is the proof. At the 920px window the brief asks about, the card has already broken — by sixty pixels — and the drawings have degraded with it for a reason worth naming: the head's height is the pile's height plus 32, so a narrower card is a shorter head, and the open column above the logo closes. At 368 it is 92 pixels tall; at the 980 break it is 13; below that there is no column at all and every constant anchored to the head's top edge lands on the logo.

Held, and so decided rather than offered: the composition — round 9's 01, not one rule of it moved; the content — the workflow's name and its connector, the schedule or trigger line, last run, next run, how many decks it has made, the ··· menu holding change, pause and delete; the card as an inert .pai-surface-raised; the count as the only link, with the trailing arrow; the closed date format — Today, 6am · 1 Sep, 6am · On a new lead; .pai-thumbnail's own contract, always 16/9 and never given a height.
